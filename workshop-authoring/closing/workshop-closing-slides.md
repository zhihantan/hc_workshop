# Unicorn Finance workshop close — slide-ready content

**Recommended duration:** 8–10 minutes
**Core slides:** 7
**Optional slide:** 1
**Audience:** Home Credit Philippines workshop participants

Use the visible content as slide copy. Presenter notes are not intended to appear on the slide.

---

## Slide 1 — At 9:00 AM: inherited assets, unanswered questions

**Priority:** Essential

### Visible content

- Atlas Ridge had delivered the Databricks platform.
- Nova Mobile's 0%-interest smartphone financing campaign increased volume.
- FPD5 appeared concentrated by store and sales associate.
- The signal was not proof of fraud or causality.
- Operating responsibility was moving to Unicorn Finance.

**Bottom line:** Assets existed. Trust and ownership still had to be established.

### Recommended visual

A handover line with Atlas Ridge Consulting on the left and the Unicorn Finance internal team on the right. Place the inherited data, notebook, dashboard, pipeline, and model assets between them. Add one unresolved-question marker above Nova Mobile's financing campaign.

### Presenter notes

Return to the opening story. Participants began as the internal team receiving an inherited platform. The problem was not a lack of technology; it was the need to understand the business definition, validate the evidence, operate the workloads, diagnose access, and own future changes.

If needed, restate that “0%” meant 0% monthly interest on selected smartphone financing—not a free phone, zero down payment, zero fees, or guaranteed approval.

Do not imply that the concentrated pattern proved fraud or that the workshop established causality.

### Transition

“We used one business question to trace the responsibilities that come with platform ownership.”

## Slide 2 — One question took us through the whole platform

**Priority:** Essential

### Visible content

```text
What happened?
      ↓
Can we trust the evidence?
      ↓
Can the logic run repeatedly?
      ↓
Can the right people use it safely?
      ↓
Can we use it to support future decisions?
```

### Recommended visual

A five-stage vertical or left-to-right journey. Label the stages:

1. Analysis
2. AI-assisted development
3. Data engineering
4. Governance
5. Machine learning

Keep the business questions visually dominant; product names should be secondary.

### Presenter notes

Explain that the sessions were not isolated feature tours:

- Data Analysis established the evidence and shared FPD5 definition.
- Generative AI accelerated understanding and improvement while preserving human review.
- Data Engineering made selected logic repeatable and observable.
- Governance established how to diagnose access and understand downstream impact.
- Machine Learning turned the governed cohort into an interpretable, operated scoring asset.

The same synthetic lending lifecycle and FPD5 definition connected the work.

### Transition

“By following that thread, we changed the state of the handover.”

## Slide 3 — By 5:00 PM: investigation became operated assets

- **Safe access:** least-privilege diagnosis, lineage, and discovery

**Bottom line:** One business question now has an end-to-end operating path.

### Recommended visual

A layered asset stack:

```text
Business decision

Dashboard, Genie, and governed metrics
Jobs, pipelines, and quality controls
Unity Catalog data, permissions, and lineage
```

Show validation, monitoring, and ownership as three vertical rails crossing every layer.

### Presenter notes

Describe outcomes, not product inventory. Use “built,” “tested,” or “observed” only for activities actually completed during the workshop. If an activity was demonstrated rather than participant-run, say so explicitly.

The important change is that evidence, controls, and ownership now travel with the assets.

“Here is the before-and-after view of that change.”

## Slide 4 — The handover changed from possession to control

**Priority:** Essential

### Visible content

**We started with**

- inherited assets and unknown dependencies;
- an investigation signal with an easy-to-misstate denominator;
- one-time analysis and manual intervention;
- access described as a single yes-or-no question;
- a model idea without an operating decision.

**We finished with**

- one governed FPD5 definition and reproducible evidence;
- reviewed changes with executable validation;
- repeatable workloads with monitoring and recovery paths;
- layered, least-privilege access diagnosis and lineage evidence;
- a tracked model, governed scores, and ownership decisions.

### Recommended visual

A clean two-column “Started with / Finished with” comparison. Use muted gray on the left and a restrained Databricks orange accent on the right. Connect each row horizontally so the transformation is obvious.

### Presenter notes

Stress that “finished” means the workshop established the method and ownership questions. It does not mean every production concern has been solved or every workshop artifact is production-ready.

Adjust any row that was not completed during the live day. Never claim a participant created, tested, or validated an asset that was skipped.

### Transition

“The tools helped, but the controls are what made the outputs trustworthy.”

## Slide 5 — Trust came from controls, not from product names

**Priority:** Essential

### Visible content

Every asset needs:

1. a business purpose and owner;
2. a governed source and definition;
3. appropriate compute and permissions;
4. an executable validation check;
5. a monitoring location;
6. a recovery or rollback action.

### Recommended visual

Place one Unicorn Finance asset at the center. Surround it with six control segments matching the list. Use examples such as the Metric View, pipeline, dashboard, or registered model, but do not crowd the slide with multiple architectures.

### Presenter notes

Connect the list to evidence from the day:

- the FPD5 denominator demonstrated the need for one definition;
- the Genie Code exercise demonstrated review and executable validation;
- Jobs and pipelines demonstrated quality, monitoring, and recovery;
- the governance demonstration separated workspace access, runtime identity, and data privileges;
- MLflow and Unity Catalog connected a model version to parameters, metrics, lineage, and governed batch outputs.

This is the reusable operating standard participants should take back to their own assets.

### Transition

“That standard tells Unicorn Finance what it must own after the partner leaves.”

## Slide 6 — What Unicorn Finance owns on Monday

**Priority:** Essential

### Visible content

- **Analysis owner:** definition, reconciliation, and stakeholder interpretation
- **Pipeline operator:** schedule, quality, alert, rerun, and recovery
- **Data steward:** grants, discovery, lineage, and downstream impact
- **Model owner:** features, evaluation, promotion, drift, and rollback
- **Platform team:** compute policies, shared services, observability, and escalation

**Shared responsibility:** changes are reviewed, validated, monitored, and documented.

### Recommended visual

Five connected ownership cards around a central “Unicorn Finance operating model” label. Avoid presenting the roles as a rigid organization chart; one team or person may hold more than one role.

### Presenter notes

The role names describe responsibilities, not required job titles. Ask participants to translate them into their real organization.

Make the handover distinction explicit: Atlas Ridge may have delivered the initial assets, but Unicorn Finance now owns business correctness, access decisions, workload operation, model use, and change management.

### Transition

“Before we finish, make that ownership concrete for one asset.”

## Slide 7 — Make the handover real

**Priority:** Essential

### Visible content

Choose one asset from today and write:

1. its business purpose;
2. its internal owner;
3. one validation check;
4. where it is monitored;
5. its first recovery action.

**Prompt:** What would prevent your team from operating this asset tomorrow?

### Recommended visual

A simple five-field handover card with an empty risk or blocker field at the bottom. Leave enough whitespace for a two-minute individual or team reflection.

### Presenter notes

Give participants 60–90 seconds to choose an analysis table, Metric View, dashboard, Genie Agent, Job, pipeline, governed table, or registered model. Then ask two or three teams to share only their biggest operating gap.

Do not turn this into a broad feedback survey. The purpose is to convert workshop learning into one actionable ownership decision.

### Transition

After responses, move to the final statement.
